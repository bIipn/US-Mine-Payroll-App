What it can do

The screens below are the app, running with a made-up company, Sample Mining Co.

Operator equipment checklist

One card for every machine: the same 21 lines, each marked OK, BO (Bad Order) or N/A. It takes the equipment hours at the start and the end, the gallons added and the comments.

  • A Bad Order on a safety item red tags the unit and shows the steps to take.
  • Where a standard applies to a line, the line cites it: the MSHA standard, and the matching OSHA rule where there is one.
  • A Bad Order has to say what is wrong before it can be signed.
  • The day is laid out as the paper card, a column for each unit checked.

Swipe for more screens.

Pre shift exams

Each work area is examined on its own sheet. Every line is checked for guarding, electrical and safe access, or marked once OK or BO where the sheet has one column. Then come the 20 conditions.

  • A condition found stays open until the date it was fixed is recorded.
  • A danger shows first wherever its area appears.
  • A condition can go to the shop as a maintenance request. Completing the request records the date it was fixed.

Maintenance requests

A request has the paper form’s boxes: By, Date, Location and Problem, then Assigned to, Date completed, Time spent, Completed by and Remarks. A supervisor assigns it, and it is not closed without remarks.

The crew board

On a supervisor’s phone, the crew board shows who is on the clock, who is at lunch or due for one, which units are down and what needs a supervisor now, from the records on that phone. Once the crew’s phones are connected, the office dashboard brings their records together.

From Bad Order to date fixed

What the crew finds on a checklist or an exam stays on the record until it is fixed, and each step is signed by the person who took it.

  1. 1Bad Order

    An operator marks a line BO on the equipment checklist, or an examiner on the pre shift exam. It cannot be signed until it says what is wrong.

  2. 2Red tag

    A Bad Order on a safety item takes the unit out of service, with a Danger, Do Not Operate tag and the steps to take.

  3. 3Maintenance request

    The Bad Order fills in the request. A supervisor assigns it.

  4. 4Completed

    The person it is assigned to, or a supervisor, completes it with the remarks and the time spent. It is not closed without remarks.

  5. 5Date fixed

    Completing the request records the date fixed on the exam it came from, and puts the unit back in service unless another request still has it tagged out.

A condition with no date fixed stays open on its area and on the supervisor’s board. Without a request, the examiner or a supervisor records the date fixed by hand. Marking the line OK on a later exam does not close it.

The PDF for an inspector lists each defect and condition with when it was corrected (30 CFR 56.14100(d) and 56.18002).

The records an inspector asks to see, in one PDF

One tap on Checklists and exams (the safety log) makes a PDF of the records an MSHA inspector asks to see, for the day or as far back as the last 12 months. On a phone it holds that phone’s records; on the office dashboard it is made from the records the crew’s phones have sent.

Time clock and payroll hours

Time tracking is optional. With it on, the same app keeps the crew’s hours and figures them the way the paper time sheet does.

Or the safety forms alone

A company can run the equipment checklist, pre shift exams, maintenance requests and the inspector PDF with no clock, hours or payroll. The price is the same either way.

Clock in at the mine

  • A clock-in made with a location goes through only inside the mine boundary. The location is read at the moment of the punch, never in the background.
  • With location off, approximate only, or no fix, a clock-in still goes through, with no location, marked for a supervisor to review; nothing is paid or docked for it.
  • Clocking out and starting lunch are never blocked by location.
  • Lunch is one tap, and it ends by itself after 30 minutes.

Swipe for more screens.

Hours to the hundredth

Minutes become hundredths of an hour, as on the paper time sheet.

  • Overtime, double time and meal rules follow the company’s state, for all 50 states and DC, where the app can apply them; the rest are shown as notes.
  • Pay periods are the company’s own, or none at all.
  • Holiday, PTO and sick hours, kept apart from overtime.
  • A correction to a punch needs a reason, and it is kept in the shift’s history.

The payroll hours file

A plain CSV with each person’s payroll ID, the company’s own earning codes and the hours (no pay amounts), for the payroll system’s import. There are Excel exports for payroll and the safety log too.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost?

$300 a month per company, with the whole crew and everything included: the safety forms, the time clock and payroll hours, the office dashboard, the inspector PDF and the exports. It starts with a 14-day free trial, once the crew’s app is set up. See Pricing for how the trial and billing work.

Can the crew download the app?

Not yet. MineMuster is built for iPhone, Android and the web, but it is not in the App Store or Google Play yet. During early access we set up the crew’s app with each new company.

Can we try it before we start?

Yes. Try the app in your browser with the made-up Sample Mining Co.; nothing you do there is sent anywhere. Then the 14-day free trial is the whole of MineMuster for your own company.

How does a company get started?

With the free trial. A supervisor creates an account on the dashboard at app.minemuster.com, starts the company, which gives it a join code, and starts the 14-day free trial. During early access we set up the crew’s app with you. Each person then signs in with an email and password, and types the code and their name as it is on the roster.

Does the crew need a signal?

Not to work. Every punch, checklist, exam and request is saved on the phone the moment it is signed. Joining the company and sending the records need signal: once a company’s crew is connected, the records go to the company’s account when the phone is back in range.

Do we have to use the time clock?

No. Time tracking is optional. A company can run the safety forms alone: the equipment checklist, pre shift exams, maintenance requests and the PDF for an inspector, with no clock, hours or payroll. The price is the same either way.

Does it track where people are?

No. The phone’s location is read only at the moment of a punch (clock in, start lunch, clock out), and when a supervisor sets the mine boundary. A punch’s location is kept with it, and the company’s supervisors can see it. A clock-in made with a location goes through only inside the mine boundary. With location off, approximate only, or no fix, it still goes through, marked for a supervisor to review; nothing is paid or docked for it. It is never read in the background, and no location is read when lunch ends.

Which state’s hours rules does it follow?

The company’s. Overtime, double time and meal rules follow the company’s state, for all 50 states and DC, where the app can apply them; a rule it cannot apply is shown as a note. A bargaining agreement can change some of them. Holiday, PTO and sick hours never count toward overtime.

Can the hours go into QuickBooks, ADP or Sage?

MineMuster makes a payroll hours file for your payroll system’s import: a plain CSV with each person’s payroll ID, your own earning codes and the hours, with no pay amounts.

QuickBooks Online imports it as time activities, mapping the columns from the first row. Sage 100 and Sage 300 CRE let you set the import layout to match it. ADP Workforce Now needs its own paydata layout, which MineMuster does not make yet.

Check an import against your own payroll account before you rely on it.

Can a supervisor fix a punch?

Yes, with a reason. A correction is added to the shift’s edit history, and punch times are never changed silently. A shift with no clock-out counts no hours until a supervisor sets its end.

Who sees a person’s hourly rate?

Only that person. A rate is entered for their own pay estimate, stays on their phone and is never in any export. The estimate is of gross pay; the pay stub is the official figure.

Which mines does the inspector PDF cover?

Surface metal and nonmetal mines, under 30 CFR Part 56. It does not cover underground mines under Part 57 or coal mines under Parts 75 and 77.

Is the standards text in the app legal advice?

No. Where a line cites a standard, the text beside it is a plain-language summary, not legal advice.
