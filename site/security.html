Where records are kept

A record is saved on the phone the moment it is signed: every punch, equipment checklist, pre shift exam and maintenance request. Nothing waits on a network.

Once a phone has joined its company, it sends the company’s records to MineMuster’s server whenever it has signal. The server is a Supabase project, a PostgreSQL database with Supabase’s sign-in, in Amazon Web Services’ us-west-1 region in Northern California. Every connection to it is HTTPS: Supabase’s project APIs accept no other kind.

The website and the office dashboard at app.minemuster.com are served by Vercel. The dashboard reads the company’s records from the server; it keeps none of them itself.

Who can read them

  • Row level security is on for every table on the server. A person signed in reads only the records of the company they belong to.
  • Only a company’s supervisors change its roster, its area sheets, paid leave and the company’s settings. Every record on the dashboard says which account sent it.
  • A name on the company’s roster joins with the join code. Anyone else waits until a supervisor lets them in or turns them down.
  • A phone sends to one company only. Records that went to one company never go to another until a supervisor clears the phone.
  • The app and the dashboard carry only the project’s publishable key, which row level security holds to these rules. Neither will take a secret key.

Signing in

Everyone signs in with an email and a password, through Supabase’s sign-in. The password goes to Supabase and nowhere else. The links in the emails to confirm an account or reset a password sign in only the browser that asked for them.

The dashboard keeps the person signed in with the browser’s own storage, and signing out signs out that browser only. Its pages may exchange data only with MineMuster’s server, and they cannot be shown inside another site’s page.

What never leaves the phone

  • The hourly rate a person enters for their own pay estimate. It is in no export, and no one else sees it.
  • The accounts and passwords on the phone.
  • The phone’s own settings, such as its lunch reminders.
  • Anything of the made-up sample crew.

The phone never sends them, and the server turns down any record that carries a rate or a password.

Location

The phone’s location is read only at the moment of a punch (clock in, start lunch, clock out) and kept with that punch. The point it was read at goes to the server with the punch, and the company’s supervisors can see it. It is never read in the background, and none is read when lunch ends. A clock-in made with a location goes through only inside the mine boundary. With location off, approximate only, or no fix, it still goes through, with no location, marked for a supervisor to review; nothing is paid or docked for it. Clocking out and starting lunch are never blocked by location.

The one other time a location is read is when a supervisor puts the boundary’s pin where they stand. A company that runs the safety forms alone, with no time clock, has no boundary and reads no location at all.

Payments

Stripe takes the card on its own pages, for the trial and the monthly charge, and the card is changed in Stripe’s billing portal. MineMuster never sees the card number. The server keeps only where each subscription stands (its status, when the trial ends, when the period paid for ends and whether it is cancelled), from messages Stripe signs; a message without Stripe’s signature is refused.

This website

minemuster.com has no analytics, no trackers and no cookies, and it loads nothing from any other site: its fonts and scripts are its own. Its pages may run only their own scripts. The one thing it sends anywhere is what you type into the contact form, which goes to MineMuster’s server when you press Send.

What we do not claim

MineMuster has not been through an outside security audit or certification, and it publishes no uptime figure. The standards text in the app is a plain-language summary, not legal advice, and the records it keeps do not stand in for a mine’s own duty to keep them. While the subscription is open, a supervisor can export the company’s records from the dashboard; export what you need to keep before you cancel. During early access, keep the paper equipment cards and pre shift exams as well, until your crew’s records are reaching the dashboard.

Reporting a problem

If you find a security problem, write to us through the contact form: say what you found and how to see it. Please do not try to reach another company’s records to show it.
