Privacy

The short version

  • This website has no analytics, no trackers and no cookies.
  • MineMuster keeps a company’s records for that company, and uses them only to run MineMuster for it. We do not sell them or use them for advertising.
  • Location is read only at the moment of a punch, and when a supervisor sets the mine boundary, never in the background. A punch’s location goes to the server with it, and the company’s supervisors can see it. With location off, approximate only, or no fix, a clock-in still goes through, with no location, marked for a supervisor to review.
  • An hourly rate a person enters stays on their phone.
  • A crew member signs in with the company code, their name and a passcode, and has no email with MineMuster. The passcode is kept only as a scrambled form, never as typed.
  • Stripe takes the card. MineMuster never sees the card number.

In this policy “MineMuster”, “we” and “us” mean MineMuster, which runs minemuster.com, the office dashboard at app.minemuster.com and the MineMuster app.

This website

minemuster.com sets no cookies and runs no analytics or trackers. It loads nothing from any other site: its fonts, images and scripts are served with its pages. Vercel serves the site, and like any web host it handles each request for a page, including the address it came from, in order to send the page back.

The demo at minemuster.com/demo is the app running with a made-up company. What you do in it is kept in your browser’s storage and sent nowhere.

The contact form

When you press Send, the form sends what you typed to MineMuster’s server: your name, your company, your email, your phone number if you gave one, and your message. We keep it there to read it and answer you, and use it for nothing else: it is not added to a mailing list. The server counts how many messages have come from an email address in the last day, and from one network address in the last hour, so the form cannot be flooded. For the hourly count it keeps only a scrambled form of the network address, never the address itself, and deletes it after an hour.

To have a message deleted, ask through the form.

The office dashboard

Signing in to app.minemuster.com takes an email and a password. Sign-in is handled by Supabase: the password goes to Supabase and nowhere else, and the emails to confirm an account, reset a password or invite a supervisor are sent through it.

A supervisor can invite another supervisor by email from the Roster page. We keep the invited address with the invitation, and when it was sent and when it was accepted or cancelled, as a record on the company’s roster. The invitation is good for 7 days, and nobody is put on the roster until the person accepts. That record, and the address in it, stays until the company deletes that roster row or closes the company, even after the invitation is accepted, cancelled or has run out.

If the invited address has no MineMuster account yet, Supabase makes one for it so the person can accept: an account that is not confirmed yet, holding the address and the company’s name. It stays until it is deleted, even if the person never accepts. The person can delete it by opening the invite link, choosing a password, signing in at app.minemuster.com and choosing Delete my account under Your account, or by asking through the contact form, as Delete your account says.

The dashboard keeps the person signed in with the browser’s own storage, not cookies. The browser also keeps which company was open last, and, for a week at most, that the person came from this website to start a free trial. Nothing else is stored on the computer. Signing out signs out that browser.

Everything the dashboard shows is read from the company’s records on MineMuster’s server (see below). It shows no one’s hourly rate or pay.

The crew app

The app saves every record on the phone the moment it is signed. Once a phone has joined its company, it sends the company’s records to MineMuster’s server whenever it has signal:

  • punches and their corrections, with the reason for each correction, and punches that were turned down;
  • paid leave;
  • equipment checklists and equipment use;
  • pre shift exams and maintenance requests;
  • the roster (names, job titles, who is a supervisor and each person’s payroll ID), the fleet and the area sheets;
  • the company’s settings: its name, state, place, MSHA mine ID, logo, pay periods, the day the workweek starts, clock-in site, earning codes, whether it keeps time, and whether a whole section of an area sheet can be marked OK at once.

These never leave the phone: the hourly rate a person enters for their own pay estimate, the accounts and passwords on the phone, the phone’s own settings (such as its lunch reminders) and anything of the made-up sample crew. The lunch reminders and the notice that lunch is over are set on the phone itself; no notification service is involved.

A crew member signs in to the company’s server with the company code, their name and a passcode. MineMuster makes each crew member’s account on the server itself, under an address made up for it that receives no mail and that nobody sees or types, so a crew member’s account holds no email. It holds a random salt and a scrambled form of the passcode (a keyed digest: the passcode itself is never kept), how many wrong tries there have been in a row, until when the account is locked, when the account was made and when the passcode was last set, and, while a reset code is open, a scrambled form of that code, when it runs out and how many tries it has had. Nothing else of MineMuster’s is kept about the account but its made-up address and the fact that it signs in with a passcode. Supabase Auth, the service the server signs people in with, also keeps a scrambled form of the account’s own password, which nobody sees, and a record of each session, with the network address and device it came from, as sign-in services do. The name is the one on the company’s roster, or on the person’s request to join until a supervisor lets them in. If someone forgets their passcode, a supervisor makes a reset code on the dashboard and shows it to them; it is kept only as the same kind of scrambled form, and is good for 30 minutes.

To slow down anyone trying many names or codes, the server counts each sign-in request by the network address it came from. It keeps only a scrambled form of that address, never the address itself, for 10 minutes; a crew sign-in request after that deletes it, almost always the very next one. Supervisors sign in with an email and a password, as on the dashboard.

The phone keeps the session so it stays signed in. The app never signs anyone out by itself. The session ends when the person signs out of the phone or deletes their account. A passcode reset signs the account out of every other phone, and a supervisor deleting the person’s sign-in on the dashboard signs it out of every phone at once.

Location

Where a company keeps time, the phone’s location is read at the moment of a punch (clock in, start lunch, clock out) and kept with that punch. A clock-in made with a location goes through only inside the mine boundary. With location off, approximate only, or no fix, it still goes through, with no location, marked for a supervisor to review; nothing is paid or docked for it. The point it was read at, how accurate the phone said it was, how far it was from the pin, whether it was inside the boundary and whether the phone said the location was simulated go to the server with the punch (and with a clock-in that was turned down), and the company’s supervisors can see them. A punch made with no location carries no position, only whether location was off, approximate only, or no fix; when the phone shares only an approximate location, no location is read at all. It is also read when a supervisor sets the mine boundary: to put its pin where they stand, or to check where they stand against it, which is shown on their screen and not kept. It is never read in the background, and none is read when lunch ends. A company that runs the safety forms alone reads no location.

Billing

A company pays through Stripe. The card and the billing details are entered on Stripe’s own pages, and Stripe charges the card; MineMuster never sees the card number. Stripe tells MineMuster’s server where the company’s subscription stands, and the server keeps only that: Stripe’s ids for the subscription and the customer, its status, when the trial ends, when the period paid for ends and whether it is cancelled. Stripe handles what it is given under its own privacy policy.

Who else handles it

Three companies run parts of MineMuster for us, and see only what that part needs:

  • Supabase runs MineMuster’s server: the database and sign-in, in Amazon Web Services’ us-west-1 region in Northern California. The company’s records, the accounts and the contact form’s messages are kept there.
  • Vercel serves this website and the office dashboard.
  • Stripe takes the payments.

We give personal information to no one else, unless the law requires it.

A company’s records

The records a crew makes in MineMuster belong to their company. The company decides who is on its roster and what goes in, and each company reads only its own records. If you work for a company that uses MineMuster and have a question about your records, ask a supervisor there first; you can also write to us.

MineMuster is for businesses and is not meant for children.

Exporting and deleting

While the subscription is open, a supervisor can export the company’s records from the dashboard: the PDF for an inspector, the Excel safety log, the payroll workbook and the payroll hours file. After it ends, the dashboard opens only its Billing page, and a supervisor’s phone can still make the inspector PDF and the safety log from the records on that phone. Export what you need to keep before you cancel.

A company can ask through the contact form to have its records deleted from the server.

Records on a phone stay on it until the app is removed, a supervisor clears the phone from the More screen, or the person whose own company sign-in the phone sends with signs out of the phone or deletes their account, each only once everything on it has reached the company.

A person deletes their own account as Deleting your account says, below.

Deleting your account

Anyone can delete their own MineMuster account: in the app, under More, then Delete my account (a crew member types their passcode again), or on the office dashboard, under Your account. A crew member who cannot sign in asks a supervisor to delete the sign-in from the dashboard, and anyone else who cannot sign in asks through the contact form. Delete your account has the steps.

  • What is deleted: the person’s company sign-in, their email and password (or, for a crew member, their passcode and the address made up for the account), for good, and their requests to join a company. On the phone they delete it from, the app’s sign-in and the hourly rate they entered are deleted too. An account they made in the app on another phone stays on that phone until it is deleted there, under More, or the app is deleted.
  • What the company keeps: the person’s name on its roster, and the punches, paid leave, equipment checklists, pre shift exams and maintenance requests they made, with their name on them. Only the link to the deleted account is removed. They are the company’s records: the company keeps pre shift exam records for at least a year, as 30 CFR 56.18002 has it, and the time and pay records the law has the company keep. MineMuster keeps them for the company while it uses MineMuster, and deletes them when the company asks.
  • A company’s last supervisor makes someone else a supervisor before deleting their account, or, when nobody else at the company can take over, closes the company on the dashboard, which deletes the company and its records. Closing waits until 30 days after the last person taken off the roster was taken off, since they could still be put back.
  • How long: in the app and on the dashboard, and when a supervisor deletes a crew sign-in, at once; through the contact form, within 30 days of the person’s reply to our email. Copies in the server’s backups are deleted when those backups expire.

An account made on a phone that never joined a company is kept on that phone alone, and MineMuster never has it. Deleting it in the app, or deleting the app, removes it.

Changes and questions
