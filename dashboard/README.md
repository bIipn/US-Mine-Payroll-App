# MineMuster office dashboard

The office side of MineMuster, for supervisors and the people in the office, served at **https://app.minemuster.com**. The crew use the app on their phones, signing in with the company code, their name and a passcode, never on the dashboard; the phones send their records to the company's server, and this dashboard reads them there.

It is a Vite + React + TypeScript web app. It talks to the MineMuster Supabase project (`supabase/` in this repository) and nothing else, and it works out every figure with the app's own code from `../src`, so the office and the phones always agree.

## What it does

**Signing in.** Email and password, with Supabase Auth, for supervisors and the office. A new account confirms its email first: after creating one, the page says to check for the link and then sign in, the same words whether or not the email already had an account, so the page tells no one which emails do. "Forgot your password?" emails a link that opens a page to choose a new one. The links use the PKCE flow: a link only signs in the browser that asked for it, so a link someone made for their own account signs nobody in here. A link that has run out, was used already or is opened in another browser says so on the sign-in page. Signing out signs out this browser only; the same account on a phone stays signed in. The password goes to Supabase and nowhere else.

**No company yet.** A new account either starts a company or joins one:

- *Start a company*: the company's name, the state whose hours rules it follows (the 50 states and DC), its time zone (this computer's by default), and the person's own name and job title. They become its first supervisor, and the page shows the company code to give the crew (`create_company`). One account starts at most 3 companies in any 24 hours and 20 in all; past either the server answers `PT429`, and the page says which limit it is and what to do (`companyLimitWords` in `src/lib/errors.ts`).
- *Join with the company code*: the code a supervisor gave out and the name as it is on the roster (`join_company`). The page says what the answer means, as `supabase/README.md` lays out: in (open the company), waiting for a supervisor (with the reason, and a button to check again), a name that fits more than one person, turned down, or no company with that code. The person's own requests are listed below.

**An invitation to supervise.** A supervisor invites another from the Roster page (see Sign-ins on the Roster page, below). The invited address gets Supabase Auth's invite email, with a link to `https://app.minemuster.com/?invited=1`, unless it already has a confirmed account: that account gets no email, and the invitation waits and shows the next time it signs in. How the page opens on the link (`readArrival` in `src/lib/supabase.ts`, `src/lib/arrival.ts`, `src/screens/InvitationsScreen.tsx`):

- An address whose hash holds `access_token` or `refresh_token` is tidied before the client is made, whatever the query says, so no session is left in the address bar. A session is taken from it only for an invitation (`invited=1` in the query, `type=invite` in the hash, both halves of the session and no code or error); every other link stays PKCE code-only, and a session in any other address is not taken.
- The first screen after an invite link names the signed-in address ("Set a password for <address>", with "This is not my email: sign out"), since anyone can write an address with a session of their own. Then the invitations waiting for this account (`my_supervisor_invitations`) come before its companies, and "Accept and open <company>" puts the account on the row as a supervisor (`accept_supervisor_invitation`), only with the invited address once Supabase Auth has confirmed it, and only while the supervisor who sent it still is one. Accepting writes the sign-in row alone, so once the company opens and its records have loaded, the page makes the person in the app's roster a supervisor too, with the same write as Make both supervisor (`finishAccepting` in `src/lib/rosterPeople.ts`, `AcceptedNotice`), and says so. With the records not loaded, or the write refused, it says that and points to Make both supervisor on their row.
- An invite link that has expired, was used already or failed says so: on the sign-in page when nobody is signed in, or, when the browser keeps another account's session, before the companies, naming that account, with "Go on as <address>" and "Sign out". An invitation accepted already uses its link up, so the same words say to go on if it was accepted. The page never signs anyone out by itself.
- There is nothing to decline: an invitation left alone ends after 7 days, and the supervisor who sent it can cancel it.

**Crew.** Crew use the app, not the dashboard. An account that holds a crew row and signs in here with email (one that signed in with email before passcodes, or whose row an invitation has yet to promote) sees its company and a short note that the dashboard is for supervisors: its hours and forms are in the app, on whose first screen it taps **Supervisor? Sign in with email** and signs in with the same email and password. New crew sign up in the app with the company code, their name and a passcode, which the Overview says too.

**Supervisors.** A dark sidebar with the company and these pages:

| Page | What is on it |

| Roster | Where people are changed: the sign-in roster from the `roster` table, and the roster in the app, the `employee` records every phone receives, written together (see The Roster page writes two rosters, below). Add one person or paste a list (`Last, First \| Job title \| supervisor`); behind each person's Change button, change a job title, make them a supervisor or crew (asked once more either way), let go of an account that joined on the wrong row, take them off (asked first, with Take off now and Cancel, when their phone has sent a shift with no clock-out from the last 16 hours); put back someone taken off. The signed-in supervisor's row says You, and the last supervisor who can sign in cannot be made crew, let go or taken off. The requests waiting, to let in (on a new row, an open row, or a row taken from the wrong account) or turn down, with or without keeping them out; keeping them out bars that sign-in alone, since anyone with the company code can sign up again, which only a new code stops, and the page says so. The company code, with a button to make a new one. An Account column and each row's sign-in tools (see Sign-ins on the Roster page, below). The rows nobody in the app is for (On the sign-in roster, not in the app), each with Add to the app's roster; the people in the app with no sign-in row, to put on it one at a time or all at once, or to take off the app's roster; the people taken off the sign-in roster who are still in the app (Off the sign-in roster, still in the app), to take off the app's roster too. For a company that keeps time, each person's payroll ID, which Time and payroll links to |
| Fleet | The company's equipment, the `equipment` records: add a unit (unit number, type, make, model, the equipment hours or miles it starts at), change a unit's type, make and model (its reading is in miles for a light vehicle and in hours for anything else, so that follows the type; the unit number stays), and take a unit out of the fleet. Each unit shows its standing (ready, defect reported, red tagged, the words every other page uses) and its last reading as the records give them; neither is changed here. Before a unit is taken out the page reads again what is open on it (a red tag, open maintenance requests, Bad Orders not corrected), says so, says that phones take the unit off the next time they are open with signal and who has it checked out (a use with no end, whose phone keeps the unit until it ends), and asks once more. Units that share a unit number are flagged, and so is a unit that was taken out here and is back because a supervisor's phone added it again |

Changing a sheet changes only the exams made from now on: a signed exam keeps the lines it was made on. A sheet with a danger open in its area comes first and is marked, in use or not. The equipment checklist is not here: it is the one printed card for every machine, 21 lines, and is not edited; a unit is on the crew's checklist once it is in the fleet on their phones |

| Defects and conditions | Any danger first, as on the overview; units down; every Bad Order on an equipment checklist not corrected yet and every condition from a pre shift exam with no date fixed, each with its request and how many days it has been open (to a tenth, as the safety log counts them); and what was corrected in the last 30 days |
| Maintenance requests | The queue (not completed, in progress, completed, all), each request opening as the paper form with where it came from and its history |
| Time and payroll | Only when the company keeps time. Hours by the company's pay period, or by workweek and month when it has none: straight time, overtime, double time, holiday, PTO and sick, meal flags for review and shifts with no clock-out, with each person's days. The payroll workbook and the hours file for the span shown |
| Company | The shared settings this dashboard and every phone go by, the one `settings` record: the company's name, state, place and MSHA mine ID, whether the crew keeps time, the pay schedule, the earning codes, the day the workweek starts and the "Rest are OK" switch, with the company's time zone beside them. What is wrong is said at the field at fault, which is marked and takes the focus. Save, and what the last save said, stay at the foot of the window. The logo and the clock-in site are shown and not changed (the pin is set standing at the mine, on a phone). When, before the server turned such copies down, a supervisor's phone sent its own copy over the settings saved here, the page says so and what that set back |

**Billing.** MineMuster is $300 a month for the company, the whole crew included. A 14-day free trial takes a card at checkout; Stripe charges it $300 when the trial ends, then each month, until the company cancels. Cancelling stops the next charge, and the dashboard stays open to the end of the period already paid for. The Billing page says all of that, and where the company stands, from the server's `company_billing(p_company_id)` (`supabase/migrations/20261117000500_billing.sql`), which keeps what Stripe's events said:

| The server says | The page says | The other supervisor pages |
|---|---|---|
| `none` | Start your 14-day free trial | The same panel, to start it |
| `trial` | Free trial until the day it ends, and the first charge, $300 on that day; or, cancelled, the day it closes with nothing charged | Open |
| `active` | The next charge and its date; or, cancelled, the day access ends | Open |
| `past_due` | The last charge did not go through: update the card. Stripe is trying it again | Open |
| `comped` | No charge for this company (let in by hand on the server); or, when a subscription started before that is still running, that it is still charged and how to cancel it | Open |
| `ended` | Why it ended, and how to start again: the trial if the company never had one, otherwise the payment link with no trial (`RESTART_LINK`), or Write to us while that link is not set. A second trial started anyway is said to be one, with the day Stripe charges it | The same panel, to start it again |

The rules are in `src/lib/billing.ts`, with tests: which states open the dashboard, the gate on each page, when the records load, the addresses on Stripe and the words for each state. Billing, Your account and Sign out always work. The lock is on the server: a supervisor reads the company's records only while it has MineMuster (`supabase/README.md`, What a supervisor reads). The dashboard follows the same answer and does not ask for the records until `company_billing` says the company is in. Only a server that has no `company_billing` at all (PostgREST's `PGRST202`, or `42883`) lets the supervisor in without that answer, and says quietly on Billing that billing is not set up on the server yet: a company is never shut out because the server is behind. A check that fails or takes over 15 seconds is not taken as a yes: each page says billing could not be checked and offers Check again. A check that fails after an answer is known keeps that answer, so a page neither shuts nor opens on one lost request.

"Start your 14-day free trial" is a link, not a call: Stripe's payment link opens in the same tab with the company's id as `client_reference_id` (how the server ties the subscription to the company) and the signed-in email as `prefilled_email`, each percent-encoded. Stripe sends the person back to `/billing?started=…`; the page then asks `company_billing` every 3 seconds for about a minute until Stripe's word has reached the server, offers no second checkout meanwhile, and after the minute says it can take a moment and offers Check again. Once it is in, it says the trial (or the subscription) has started, to put the crew on the Roster page, and that during early access the crew's app is set up with the company: the iPhone and Android apps are not in the App Store or Google Play yet, and the Overview says the same while no records have arrived. A company with more than one subscription running (two supervisors who each finished checkout, say) is told it is charged for each, and to cancel one in Manage billing or write to us. "Manage billing" opens Stripe's billing portal sign-in in a new tab, with the email filled in. The billing state is asked again when the person comes back to the tab, at most every 30 seconds. The card is entered on Stripe's own pages; the dashboard never sees it.

A new company opens on Billing once it is made. "Start free trial" on minemuster.com opens `https://app.minemuster.com/?start=trial`: the dashboard opens on Create an account, keeps the wish in this browser for a week at most (through confirming the email, which may open another tab), and opens on Billing when a company opens for a supervisor. The crew never see billing, and their screen never asks for it.

**Your account.** Anyone signed in deletes their own MineMuster account here, at `/account`: from Your account beside Sign out in the menu, and in the top band on the crew's page and the page of an account in no company. It opens whatever billing says (`gate` lets it through, as it does Billing), since anyone may delete their own account. It asks the server's `account_deletion_check` what deleting means in each company and says, before anything is deleted, what goes, what stays with each company and why. The plan is `src/lib/accountPlan.ts`, with tests; the page is `src/screens/AccountPage.tsx` (`AccountView` draws it, and is what the tests render). Where the person is a company's only supervisor:

| The server says | The page |
|---|---|
| Someone there can take over now (`successors`) | Names them, and sends to that company's Roster page to make one a supervisor; it writes nothing of the roster itself. No delete until then |
| Only people taken off the roster, released or deleted in the last 30 days stand in the way (`close_from`) | Says from when the company can be closed, and sends to its Roster page meanwhile |
| Nobody else could take over (`can_close`), and a subscription is still charged | Sends to that company's Billing to cancel it first |
| Nobody else could take over, and nothing is charged | The company is closed with the account: its name is typed, capitals and spacing aside, and Exports is a click away to download what it has to keep first |

The password is typed again, signed in with just before the call (the `delete-account` function deletes an account only within ten minutes of its password), then `functions.invoke('delete-account')` closes the companies named and deletes the account (`supabase/README.md`, Deleting an account). An answer that does not say whether the account went is checked with Supabase Auth first (`settleDeletion`, shared with the phone). Once it is gone the browser is signed out and the sign-in page says the account is deleted. An account made in the app on a phone stays on that phone, and the page says so.

Assigning, starting and completing a maintenance request, recording a date fixed, correcting a punch and entering paid leave stay in the app, where the rules for who may do them already live. The dashboard shows them as the phones sent them.

## What it writes

Besides the sign-in roster, the dashboard writes the four kinds of the company's records every phone receives: `employee` on the Roster page, `equipment` on Fleet, `areaSheet` on Exam sheets and the shared `settings` on Company. It writes them the way a supervisor's phone sends them, through the server's `push_records(company_id, changes)` (`supabase/README.md`), and nothing else: no shift, checklist, exam, request or leave record is ever written from here.

- **The rules are the app's own.** How a person is added or brought back, what makes a payroll ID right and which person a sign-in row is for, what makes a unit number taken, how a new unit starts, what a sheet's id is and that no two sheets share a name, how a company is tidied and checked: each is a pure function in `../src/domain` (`roster.ts`, `fleet.ts`, `areaExam.ts`, `company.ts`, `companyForm.ts`) that the app's store and screens call too. The Company page fills in the same form as the app's Company screen and gets the same company from it.
- **The same record, the same version.** A change is `{ key: "kind:id", kind, id, rev, base?, record }`. `rev` is the app's version hash (`revOf` in `src/domain/sync.ts`), `base` the version this page loaded, and a record taken off goes as `rev: "deleted"` with `record: null`. A record put back over one taken off goes with no base, as from a phone that took the deletion: the server turns down a change made from a copy that was taken off, and takes this one from a supervisor. A new person gets an id from the app's `uid('emp_')`, a new unit from its `uid`; a sheet's id is the slug of its name, so the same area typed on a phone is the same record. A test makes each change and checks it is the one `changesToSend` makes for the same record.
- **Nothing is written over a version this page has not seen.** A form saves from the version it opened on. Before a record already on the server is changed it is read again; if a phone has sent a newer version since, nothing is sent: the page says what changed, in the open form or panel where the button was pressed, shows the newer version, and puts it into every field of the form nobody touched, for the supervisor to look again and save if the change is still wanted. A form with nothing typed in it moves on to the version the page holds, so loading the records again does not stop the next save, and a button beside a record as it is drawn (stop using a sheet) goes from the version on the page. Should a version arrive in the moment between that read and the save, the server writes the save and says so (`conflict`), and the page says to look at it again.
- **"Accepted" is not taken for written.** `push_records` answers accepted, and writes nothing, when a change has the version and the base of the caller's own latest change to the record: its rule for a batch sent again after a lost answer. Versions are hashes of the record, so a real second change matches too once someone else has put the record back in between (one supervisor stops a sheet, another starts it again, the first stops it once more). So the record is read once more after every save. Since 20261120000100 the server writes the four kinds phones receive only on top of the version it holds, so no other base gets such a change written from the same account: when the server still holds the version it held before, the page says the server took the change for this account's earlier one, which someone else has since put back, that nothing was saved, and that another supervisor can make it (`repeatWords` in `src/lib/changes.ts`). It does not send it again, and does not say someone else changed the record since the page loaded it. Only a version the server shows is called saved; anything else is said as it is. A change id in the protocol would let the same account make the change; that is a change to the server.
- **A press again after a lost answer is the same record.** A new unit keeps its id until it is saved or its number is changed, and a record the server already holds exactly as it is being sent counts as saved, so a second press never adds a second unit.
- **What is open on a unit is read again before it is taken out.** A maintenance request or a Bad Order a phone sent after the page loaded is a record of its own, not a change to the unit's, so the question is built from the requests and checklists the server has at that moment.
- **The server's refusals are said in plain words** (`src/lib/changes.ts`): only a supervisor (and whether the save was adding, changing or taking out), something that never leaves a phone, a record already there under the same id, too large, the day's limit. An account no longer on the roster is told so.
- **Billing.** `push_records` takes a record whatever billing says, so that a phone never loses one. These pages are for a company that has MineMuster: they sit behind the same gate as the others, and each save asks `company_billing` first and sends nothing when the company is not in.
- **The page shows what was saved** without loading every record again: the row the server now holds takes the place of the one the page had. What was said about a save is brought into view where the person is, and takes the keyboard focus when the button that was pressed is gone from the page.
- **The company's sign-in details on the server** (`companies`: name, state, time zone, pay period) are brought in step with the settings where the two overlap, after the settings are saved.

**How phones get it.** Phones receive the company's roster, fleet, area sheets and settings: what is saved on these four pages reaches each phone the next time the app is open there with signal. The server writes a change of these four kinds only on top of the version it was made from (`supabase/migrations/20261120000100_office_changes_reach_phones.sql`), so a phone that has not caught up cannot put its older copy back over the office's, and a unit's status is never written better than the company's open maintenance requests on it: the Fleet page saves a unit at least as marked as its open requests make it (`withRequestFloor` in `src/lib/setup.ts`, by the app's own `statusFromRequests`). Each of the Fleet, Exam sheets and Company pages says so at its head, in a notice titled "How the crew's phones get what is saved here", and the line under each form says "Saved to the company. Phones get it the next time they are open with signal." A test (`test/words.test.ts`) reads every string and comment of the pages and fails on any that still says phones do not receive.

- **A unit** added here is on the crew's checklist once their phones have it. Added both here and on a supervisor's phone it is two records, because each gets an id of its own: the Fleet page flags units that share a number and shows which one the checklists are on, so the other can be taken out. A unit taken out here goes off each phone the next time it is open with signal; a phone with the unit checked out keeps it until the use ends, and the question before a unit is taken out names who has it.
- **A sheet** stopped here stops being offered on the phones; while a condition found in it is still open, its area stays on the list of the phone that found it, not in use, so a danger is still shown there, and a person in the middle of an exam on it finishes on the sheet as they saw it.
- **The settings** saved here are what every phone goes by. Saving here never takes a clock-in pin off a phone: the pin is set standing at the mine, on a supervisor's phone. The Company and Exam sheets pages still say when, before the server turned stale copies down, a supervisor's phone's copy replaced what the office saved (`writtenOver` in `src/lib/history.ts`), and what that set back and is still so.
- A unit or a sheet a supervisor's phone adds again after it was taken out here is back (a phone that took the deletion has no version to send it from, and the server takes a supervisor's word for a new one); the Fleet page lists such units.

Phones do not receive time, checklists, exams or requests, and no page says they do.

**The Roster page writes two rosters.** A phone joined to a company does not change people: they are added, taken off and given a role, a job title or a payroll ID on the Roster page, which writes the sign-in row and the person in the app (`src/lib/rosterPeople.ts`, with `addedPerson`, `payrollIdProblem` and `personForRow` from `src/domain/roster.ts`). The person goes through the same `saveRecord` and `push_records` as the other pages, with `revOf` and the version the page loaded as `base`. The two are written in an order that never says the wrong thing when the second fails, and whatever is left half done is said with what finishes it. "Saved. Phones get it…" is said only when a person was written: a change to a sign-in row alone reaches no phone.

Which person a row is for is `matchRows` (`src/lib/rosterTools.ts`), one person to a row, matched the way a phone matches a sign-in to a person: the row's name, as if typed, against the people (`personForRow`). So a row "Riley Quinn" is the person "Riley J Quinn", and a row "Riley J Quinn" is never the person "Riley Quinn": a phone joined on that row would not take that sign-in for them. A row whose name only fits someone does not get a person another row names exactly or fits as well ("Sam Ortiz" is not "Sam Ortiz Jr" while the son has a row of his own), nor one whose own row, by their exact name, was taken off. Rows written exactly alike are one person's. A row two people in the app are called by is marked Two in the app, changes nobody in the app, and lists both people with Take off the app's roster. Everyone else on the app's roster that no active row is for is in one of the two lists below the table, so everyone on the phones can be found, and taken off, on the page. The table, the two lists and the Overview's count all go by it, so nobody is in two of them.

- *Add* (one person or a pasted list): the sign-in rows first, then each person in the app, a new one under `uid('emp_')` with no payroll ID. A name the sign-in roster has already is turned down, one person as well as a list (a second row of a name and nobody could join under it); one taken off is put back on instead. Someone the app has already gets only their row, and the page says so ("In the app already as …" when the name only fits them). When someone taken off the roster fits the name, the page asks first, "Bring them back, or add a new person?", with the question, not an answer, taking the focus: brought back, they keep their id, their payroll ID and their records, and take the row's role and job title, none included. A name two people in the app have is turned down. A row added and a person not: "… is on the sign-in roster but not in the app yet, so phones do not have them. Add them to the app's roster to finish.", with Add to the app's roster beside it and the server's reason (without its "Nothing was saved"), and the row is listed under On the sign-in roster, not in the app. Each name's new id is kept until its person is saved (`keptIds`), and each button runs once at a time (`oneAtATime`), so a second press or a try again never makes a second person of the name. A row the sign-in roster named for someone's hours before the app had them adds the person under the id those hours carry, an answer of Add a new person included. Letting a request in on a new row writes its person after it the same way.
- *Change* a role or a job title, or put a row back on: the person first, from the version the page loaded, then the row. The person turned down saves nothing ("Someone else changed … since this page loaded it."); the person saved and the row not is said as changed in the app and not on the sign-in roster, to change again.
- *Take off*: the person off the app's roster first (`active: false`, never deleted), then the row removed, or switched off when an account joined on it. A person another row is theirs too stays in the app. Someone on the clock, as far as their phone has sent, is asked about first, for a company that keeps time (a shift open longer than the state's rules give a clock-out is a clock-out never sent): their clock-out reaches the company only while they are on the roster. The people in the app with no row, and those whose row was taken off, can be taken off the app's roster alone, below the table.
- *Payroll IDs*: one field for each person in the app, checked by the app's own rule (up to 20 letters and numbers, no two people the same). Only a supervisor's phone holds them; a crew account never reads them. Time and payroll's "people with hours have no payroll ID yet" links to them.
- Nothing about a person is written while the company's records are not loaded: the page could not tell who is in the app. Taking someone off and making a supervisor crew still go ahead on the sign-in roster alone, since what a sign-in can see must not wait for a load, and the page says the app's roster was not changed and how to finish once it loads.

**Sign-ins on the Roster page.** What a supervisor does about how people get in is here, not in the app (`src/lib/signIns.ts`, `RosterPage.tsx`, with `roster_sign_ins`, `make_passcode_reset_code` and the `supervisor-tools` function):

- *The Account column* says how each row is held. `roster_sign_ins` gives, for each row, `roster_id`, `sign_in` (`none`, `passcode` or `email`), `locked_until` (while 5 wrong tries keep a passcode account locked), `needs_reset` (10 wrong tries), and the row's open, unexpired invitation: `invitation_id`, `invited_email`, `invited_at` and `invitation_lapsed` (true when the supervisor who sent it is no longer a supervisor, so it cannot be accepted until it is sent again). The words are Not joined yet, Joined with a passcode, Joined with email, Locked until a time, Locked, needs a reset, Invited, waiting to accept (or, when it has lapsed, that it needs to be sent again because the supervisor who sent it is no longer one), with where an open invitation went. Without the sign-ins (not loaded, or the call failed) the row's own claim says Joined or Not joined yet. The page reads them again after every action here, whatever came of it.
- *Reset passcode*, on a row a passcode account holds, makes a 6-digit reset code, shown once, in groups of three, with what to tell the person ("In the app, tap Forgot your passcode? and type this code with a new passcode."). It is good for 30 minutes and 5 tries, works even while the account is locked, and replaces the one before.
- *Delete sign-in*, on the same rows, asks first and then deletes that crew sign-in for good (`delete_crew_sign_in`): its row and every record stay with the company, the link to the account is cleared, and every session of the account ends at once. The person can sign up for the row again with the company code. It is also the fix when someone signed up under a coworker's name.
- *Invite by email*, on a supervisor's row with no email account and no open invitation, takes an address and checks it as strictly as the database and Supabase Auth do before anything is sent. The answer is always "Invitation sent to <address>", in the same words whether or not the address has an account, so the page tells no one which emails do: someone new to MineMuster gets an email with a link, and someone who already signs in gets none, the invitation waiting for them when they sign in, so the inviter tells them to look. It says the invitation is good for 7 days, and nothing of how long the email's own link lasts. An invitation is good for 7 days, a row has one open at a time (a new one cancels the one before), and nobody is on the row until the invited person accepts.
- *Send again* gives an open invitation 7 days more and sends the email again, and *Cancel invitation* (asked first) closes it. Sending it again by a supervisor vouches for it anew.
- *Make a supervisor* on a row a passcode account holds says that supervisors sign in with email and opens the invite, since a passcode account can never hold a supervisor's row. Accepting lets the passcode account go and makes the row a supervisor's, with the invited email's account on it. A sign-in off the roster sends nothing, so the words tell the inviter to have the person send everything from their phone first (Send and receive now), and to wait or cancel the invitation until it has all gone; once they accept, the person signs out of the phone and signs in again with email, best when not on the clock. The phone's off-the-roster card says the same to them, and promises no sign-out while it holds records that sign-in can no longer send.
- The server answers the roster's refusals in plain words (`src/lib/errors.ts`), including `supervisor_needs_email` for a role change that would give a passcode account a supervisor's row. A missing row or invitation, or one of a company the caller does not supervise, is the same 404 `not_found`.

## Where the figures come from

The dashboard loads every record of the company from the `records` table, up to a thousand a request, in pages cut on the primary key (kind, then id), which never changes: a record a phone sends again while the pages load keeps its place, so none is skipped, and the reading goes on until a page comes back empty, so a server that caps a request lower still gives every row (`loadRecords` in `src/lib/api.ts`). It shapes them into the `ReportData` the app's own code takes (`shapeRecords` in `src/lib/records.ts`). From there it is the app's code that works out everything:

- Payroll: `computePayroll` with the company's state rules (`rulesFor`), overtime over whole workweeks, the company's pay periods or workweeks and months (`spans`, `payPeriod`).
- The checklist card and the exam sheet: `dailyCards`, `dayClock`, `examFindings`, `openFindings`, the 21 lines of the card and the 20 conditions as printed (`templates`, `areaExam`).
- Files: `payrollReport` and `safetyReport` (Excel), `payrollHoursFile` (CSV), `inspectorReport` (PDF).

A test makes the payroll, both workbooks, the hours file and the PDF from records shaped this way and from the app's own records, and checks they come out the same: the hours file and the PDF byte for byte, the workbooks file by file inside.

A record the phones took off is left out. A record that is not the shape the app writes, checked all the way down (every list, every line of it, every status and priority: `src/lib/validate.ts`), is set aside and counted on the overview, so one bad row never stops a page; any member of a company can send a record, so this matters. Each page is drawn inside its own error boundary as well, so a page that still cannot be drawn says so and the sidebar keeps working.

The company settings are the shared `settings` record, as it is: the name, the state and the pay periods the phones figure their hours and name their files by, whether a supervisor's phone sent it or the Company page saved it. A state left empty on the phones stays empty here too, so both figure the hours under the federal rules, and the pages say so. Until there is a settings record, the company row on the server is used, and Time and payroll and Exports say which workweek and earning codes that means.

A unit is red tagged while its stored status says so or while an open red-tag request names it, the rule the app applies to its own requests: each unit is whichever phone's copy came last, and a phone that never heard of a tag sends its copy as ready. The Fleet page goes one step further, because a unit's own record can lag behind its checklists (a phone's change to a unit is taken only on top of the company's latest version of it): there a unit is also red tagged while a Bad Order on a safety item of one of its checklists is not corrected, has a defect reported while any other Bad Order or request is open on it, and is ready only with none (`standings` in `src/lib/setup.ts`). The tag in the table, the counts and what is said before a unit is taken out go by that one rule.

A person whose records arrived before their employee record is named from the sign-in roster row of the account that sent them, and their hours are never dropped from payroll or the workbook for want of a name; the hours file, which a payroll system imports, leaves them out until their employee record (with its payroll ID) arrives, and the page lists them. Someone with no name anywhere reads "Unknown", as the app prints them. Someone taken off the app's roster is left out of payroll as on the phones, and the page lists them when they have hours in the span.

The server takes a crew member's shift, checklist or exam for anyone in the company, and any member's work on a maintenance request assigned to someone, completing it included (the work on one nobody is assigned to is a supervisor's: `supabase/migrations/20261117000900_request_completion.sql`). So every record page says which account sent it, and a record sent from a crew member's account for someone else, or a request completed from an account that is neither a supervisor nor the person it is assigned to, is listed on the overview and marked on its page for a supervisor to look at.

**Never here:** an hourly rate or anyone's pay. The rate stays on the phone it was entered on, and no page, file or test in this folder reads one. The one figure in dollars is MineMuster's own price, on Billing. Standards are cited only where the app cites them, and the app's summaries of them are plain language, not legal advice.

**Time zone.** The app's code counts days, workweeks and pay periods in the local time of the device it runs on. On a phone at the mine that is the mine's. In the office it is this computer's, so when the computer keeps a different clock from the company's time zone, every page says so. Run payroll on a computer set to the company's time zone.

## Run it

Node 22.12 or newer (`engines` in `package.json` says so, and Vercel builds with it).

```bash
cd dashboard
npm install
npm run dev          # http://localhost:5173
npm test             # Vitest: loading the records, their checks and shaping, the overview, payroll, who sent what, ranges, the roster tools and the Roster page's two writes, join answers, errors, time zones, billing, the changes it writes, the pages that write them, what they say about the phones, and what a record's versions say about a phone's copy going over the office's
npm run typecheck
npm run build        # typecheck, then the static site in dist/
npm run preview      # serve dist/ locally
```

It talks to the MineMuster Supabase project by default. To point a build at another project, copy `.env.example` to `.env.local` and set `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` (and change `connect-src` in `vercel.json`). A publishable key is meant to be in a web page; row level security on the server is what keeps one company's records from another's. A secret key or a service-role key would get past it, so the build stops if `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` is one. Git ignores every `.env` file but the example.

Until the server's record store (`records`, `push_records`, `my_companies`) is applied to the project, signing in works and the dashboard says the server is not set up for the records yet.

## How it uses the app's code

`vite.config.ts` and `tsconfig.json` point `@app` at `../src`. Only the app's pure code is used: `src/domain`, `src/util/format.ts` and `src/util/hash.ts`, and the PDF, Excel and CSV writers in `src/services`. It builds from a clean install of this folder alone, which is how Vercel builds it:

- A bare import made by a file in `../src` (`fflate`, for the Excel writer) is resolved from `dashboard/node_modules`, not the app's, which is not installed when only the dashboard is built.
- Every file, `../src` included, is compiled with this folder's `tsconfig.json` (`tsconfig` in `vite.config.ts`). Left to find the nearest one, Vite would read the app's, which extends Expo's, and Expo is not installed here.
- If a file in `../src` ever imports React Native, Expo or anything else that only runs on a phone, the build stops and says which file. `test/sharedCode.test.ts` walks every import the dashboard makes into `../src`, and every import those files make, and fails on the same thing.

The one file from `src/components` is `logoArt.ts`, the outlines of a company's built-in logo, which is data. It is loaded only for a company that uses it.

## Deploy on Vercel

1. In Vercel, add a new project from this repository.
2. Set **Root Directory** to `dashboard`. Leave on "Include files outside the root directory in the Build Step" (on by default): the build reads `../src`.
3. `vercel.json` sets the rest: framework Vite, install `npm ci`, build `npm run build`, output `dist`, every path rewritten to `index.html` so an address such as `/requests/abc` opens the dashboard on that page, long caching for the hashed files in `/assets`, and the security headers: a Content-Security-Policy that lets the page run only its own scripts and styles and talk only to the MineMuster Supabase project, a Permissions-Policy that turns off the camera, microphone and location, Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy, HSTS, and headers that keep the page out of frames. Stripe's checkout and billing portal are opened as pages (a link), never fetched, so the policy names no Stripe address. A build pointed at another project with `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` must name that project in the policy's `connect-src` too, or every call to it is blocked.
4. Under the project's Domains, add `app.minemuster.com`, and add the DNS record Vercel shows for it (a CNAME for `app`) where minemuster.com's DNS is kept.
5. In the Supabase project, under Authentication → URL Configuration, make sure Supabase Auth allows `https://app.minemuster.com` and `https://app.minemuster.com/?invited=1` as redirects. With `https://app.minemuster.com` as the Site URL (set it there if the dashboard is where accounts start), both are allowed already: Supabase Auth allows any address with the Site URL's scheme and host. With any other Site URL, add both to the redirect URLs. The second is where the invite email's link comes back; without it Supabase Auth sends the link to the Site URL, without `invited=1`, and the page does not take it as an invitation. The confirmation email and the password-reset email send people back to the address they signed up from, and Supabase only sends them to allowed addresses. Keep Confirm email on (Authentication → Sign In / Providers → Email): only an account whose address Supabase Auth has confirmed can accept an invitation, and with confirmation off every address counts as confirmed the moment someone signs up with it.

No environment variables are needed for the MineMuster project. Set the two `VITE_` settings in Vercel only to point a deployment at another one.

## Privacy

The repository is public. Every name in the tests is made up, like the app's sample crew; the fixtures are made from the app's own made-up sample data. Keep real names, a mine's location and anything else personal out of this folder, its tests and its screenshots. The browser keeps the Supabase session and, for convenience, which company was open last, and for a week at most that the person came from the website to start a trial; nothing else is stored on the computer.
